## 1. Classical vs. Quantum Optimization Paradigms

Classical optimization algorithms (e.g., Gradient Descent, Branch-and-Cut, Dynamic Programming) operate entirely within deterministic or stochastic classical state spaces:
- Parameter updates are governed by Euclidean gradients or discrete combinatorial branches:
  $$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \nabla f(\theta_t)$$
- High-dimensional combinatorial problems suffer from exponential state explosion ($2^n$).

Quantum optimization leverages the linear algebraic structure of Hilbert space $(\mathbb{C}^{2^n})$ through **Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs)**:
- An $n$-qubit quantum register represents an exponentially large superposition of $2^n$ basis states:
  $$|\psi(\theta)\rangle = \sum_{z \in \{0, 1\}^n} c_z(\theta) |z\rangle, \quad \sum_{z} |c_z(\theta)|^2 = 1$$
- Variational quantum algorithms (QAOA, VQE) formulate optimization as finding the ground state (minimum expectation value) of an observable Hamiltonian $H$:
  $$E(\theta) = \langle \psi(\theta) | H | \psi(\theta)\rangle$$
- A hybrid classical-quantum loop evaluates quantum expectations (via statevector or measurement shots) while a classical optimizer (COBYLA, BFGS, SPSA) updates the circuit angles $\theta$.

### Quantum Simulation vs. Quantum Hardware Execution
- **Quantum Simulation (Local Statevector / Qiskit)**: Simulates the $2^n$-dimensional statevector using classical matrix-vector multiplication. It provides exact, noise-free expectation values and enables ground truth verification against exact numerical diagonalization. It scales up to $\approx 20–30$ qubits on classical RAM ($\mathcal{O}(2^n)$ memory).
- **Quantum Hardware Execution (QPU)**: Operates on physical qubits using microwave or laser pulses. Expectation values must be estimated through discrete projective measurement shots ($S$), introducing Poisson/shot noise ($\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{S})$) and physical hardware noise (depolarizing, dephasing, gate infidelities, and readout errors).

## 2. Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs)

A Parameterized Quantum Circuit consists of an initial reference state $|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$ followed by alternating layers of parameterized single-qubit rotations and multi-qubit entangling operations:
$$U(\theta) = \prod_{l=1}^L \left( W_l \cdot \prod_{q=1}^n R(\theta_{l, q}) \right)$$

### 2.1 Rotation Generators
Single-qubit rotation gates are generated by Pauli matrices $\sigma \in \{X, Y, Z\}$:
$$R_\sigma(\theta) = \exp\left( -i \frac{\theta}{2} \sigma \right) = \cos\left( \frac{\theta}{2} \right) I - i \sin\left( \frac{\theta}{2} \right) \sigma$$
Explicit matrix forms:
$$R_x(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\theta/2) & -i \sin(\theta/2) \\ -i \sin(\theta/2) & \cos(\theta/2) \end{bmatrix}, \quad R_y(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\theta/2) & -\sin(\theta/2) \\ \sin(\theta/2) & \cos(\theta/2) \end{bmatrix}, \quad R_z(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} e^{-i \theta / 2} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{i \theta / 2} \end{bmatrix}$$

### 2.2 Entangling Gates
Controlled-NOT ($CX$) gates entangle neighboring qubits, creating non-separable quantum superpositions:
$$CX |00\rangle = |00\rangle, \quad CX |01\rangle = |01\rangle, \quad CX |10\rangle = |11\rangle, \quad CX |11\rangle = |10\rangle$$

### 2.3 Circuit Depth & Expressibility
- **Circuit Depth**: The longest sequence of gate operations along any qubit wire.
- **Hardware-Efficient Ansatz (HEA)**: Employs alternating layers of single-qubit rotations ($R_y, R_z$) and nearest-neighbor linear CNOT entanglers. For $n$ qubits and $L$ layers, the total number of parameters is:
  $$N_{\text{params}} = n \cdot K \cdot (L + 1)$$
  where $K$ is the number of rotation gates per qubit per layer (typically $K=2$ for $R_y-R_z$).

## 3. Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA)

QAOA (Farhi et al., 2014) is a variational quantum algorithm designed to find approximate solutions to combinatorial optimization problems encoded as Ising spin glasses.

### 3.1 Max-Cut Formulation
Given an undirected graph $G = (V, E)$ with edge weights $w_{uv} > 0$, the goal is to partition vertices $V$ into two subsets maximizing the weight of severed edges:
$$C(x) = \sum_{(u, v) \in E} w_{uv} \left( x_u (1 - x_v) + x_v (1 - x_u) \right), \quad x_u \in \{0, 1\}$$
Using the spin mapping $x_u = \frac{1 - s_u}{2}$ where $s_u \in \{-1, +1\}$:
$$C(s) = \sum_{(u, v) \in E} \frac{w_{uv}}{2} (1 - s_u s_v)$$
Promoting classical spins to Pauli-$Z$ operators:
$$H_C = \sum_{(u, v) \in E} \frac{w_{uv}}{2} (I - Z_u Z_v)$$

### 3.2 Mixer Hamiltonian
To transition between computational basis states, QAOA uses the transverse-field mixer Hamiltonian:
$$H_M = \sum_{u \in V} X_u$$

### 3.3 Variational State & Unitaries
Starting from the uniform superposition $|+\rangle^{\otimes n} = H^{\otimes n} |0\rangle^{\otimes n}$:
$$|\psi(\gamma, \beta)\rangle = \left( \prod_{l=1}^p e^{-i \beta_l H_M} e^{-i \gamma_l H_C} \right) |+\rangle^{\otimes n}$$
1. **Cost Unitary $e^{-i \gamma H_C}$**:
   $$e^{-i \gamma H_C} = \prod_{(u, v) \in E} e^{-i \gamma \frac{w_{uv}}{2} (I - Z_u Z_v)} \sim \prod_{(u, v) \in E} e^{i \frac{\gamma w_{uv}}{2} Z_u Z_v}$$
   Implemented using a $CX(u, v) \to R_z(-\gamma w_{uv}, v) \to CX(u, v)$ sequence.
2. **Mixer Unitary $e^{-i \beta H_M}$**:
   $$e^{-i \beta H_M} = \prod_{u \in V} R_x(2 \beta, u)$$

### 3.4 Approximation Ratio & Classical Bound
$$\alpha = \frac{\langle \psi(\gamma^*, \beta^*) | H_C | \psi(\gamma^*, \beta^*) \rangle}{C_{\max}}$$
As depth $p \to \infty$, by the adiabatic theorem, $|\psi(\gamma, \beta)\rangle$ approaches the exact ground state, achieving $\alpha \to 1.0$.

## 4. Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE)

VQE (Peruzzo et al., 2014) estimates the ground state energy $E_0$ of a physical or chemical Hamiltonian $H$.

### 4.1 Variational Principle
For any normalized trial wave function $|\psi(\theta)\rangle$:
$$E(\theta) = \frac{\langle \psi(\theta) | H | \psi(\theta) \rangle}{\langle \psi(\theta) | \psi(\theta) \rangle} \ge E_0$$
Equality holds if and only if $|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ is an exact ground state eigenstate.

### 4.2 Spin Chain Hamiltonians
1. **Transverse-Field Ising Model (TFIM)**:
   $$H_{\text{TFIM}} = -J \sum_{i=0}^{n-2} Z_i Z_{i+1} - g \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} X_i$$
2. **Heisenberg XXZ Spin Chain**:
   $$H_{\text{XXZ}} = \sum_{i=0}^{n-2} J \left( X_i X_{i+1} + Y_i Y_{i+1} + \Delta Z_i Z_{i+1} \right)$$

### 4.3 Exact Diagonalization Benchmark
The Hamiltonian is converted to its full $2^n \times 2^n$ Hermitian matrix $H_{\text{mat}} = \sum c_j P_j$. Exact ground truth energy is obtained by numerical eigenvalue decomposition:
$$E_0 = \min(\operatorname{eigvalsh}(H_{\text{mat}}))$$
VQE energy error is computed as $|E_{\text{VQE}} - E_0|$.

## 5. Quantum Gradients: Parameter-Shift Rule & SPSA

### 5.1 Parameter-Shift Rule
For an expectation value $f(\theta) = \langle 0 | U^\dagger(\theta) H U(\theta) | 0 \rangle$ where a gate $U_j(\theta_j) = \exp(-i \frac{\theta_j}{2} G)$ has a generator $G$ with two distinct eigenvalues $\pm r$ (e.g. Pauli matrices with $r = 1/2$):
The exact analytical derivative is:
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial \theta_j} = r \left[ f\left(\theta + \frac{\pi}{4r} e_j\right) - f\left(\theta - \frac{\pi}{4r} e_j\right) \right]$$
For single-qubit Pauli rotations ($r = 1/2, \frac{\pi}{4r} = \frac{\pi}{2}$):
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial \theta_j} = \frac{f\left(\theta + \frac{\pi}{2} e_j\right) - f\left(\theta - \frac{\pi}{2} e_j\right)}{2}$$

**Validity Conditions**:
1. The gate generator $G$ must have an eigenvalue difference $\Delta \lambda = 1$ ($G^2 = I$).
2. The parameter $\theta_j$ must appear linearly in the generator exponent.
3. Does **not** require infinitesimal perturbations; macroscopic shifts $\pm \pi/2$ avoid numerical roundoff and catastrophic cancellation.

### 5.2 Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA)
For a parameter vector of dimension $d$, calculating the full gradient via the parameter-shift rule requires $2d$ circuit evaluations per iteration. For $d = 100$, this requires 200 circuit evaluations per step.

SPSA (Spall, 1998) estimates the gradient in arbitrary dimension $d$ using only **2 function evaluations**:
1. Sample random perturbation vector $\Delta_k \in \{-1, +1\}^d$ (independent Bernoulli trials).
2. Evaluate at two perturbed points:
   $$y_k^+ = f(\theta_k + c_k \Delta_k), \quad y_k^- = f(\theta_k - c_k \Delta_k)$$
3. Compute simultaneous stochastic gradient estimate:
   $$g_k = \frac{y_k^+ - y_k^-}{2 c_k} \Delta_k^{-1} = \frac{y_k^+ - y_k^-}{2 c_k} \Delta_k$$
4. Update parameters:
   $$\theta_{k+1} = \theta_k - a_k g_k$$
   with decaying gain sequences $a_k = \frac{a}{(A + k + 1)^\alpha}$, $c_k = \frac{c}{(k + 1)^\gamma}$.

## 6. Quantum Natural Gradient (QNG)

Standard Gradient Descent assumes an isotropic Euclidean geometry on parameter space $\mathbb{R}^d$. However, moving a distance $\|\Delta \theta\|_2$ does not produce a uniform change in the quantum state $|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ due to non-linear gate parameterizations.

### 6.1 Fubini-Study Metric Tensor
The distance between two nearby quantum states is given by the Fubini-Study metric (infidelity):
$$ds^2 = 1 - |\langle \psi(\theta) \mid \psi(\theta + d\theta) \rangle|^2 = \sum_{i, j} g_{ij}(\theta) d\theta_i d\theta_j$$
The metric tensor $g_{ij}(\theta)$ is the real part of the Quantum Geometric Tensor:
$$g_{ij}(\theta) = \operatorname{Re}\left( \langle \partial_i \psi(\theta) \mid \partial_j \psi(\theta) \rangle - \langle \partial_i \psi(\theta) \mid \psi(\theta) \rangle \langle \psi(\theta) \mid \partial_j \psi(\theta) \rangle \right)$$

### 6.2 Regularized Riemannian Update
Because quantum circuits often exhibit parameter redundancies (e.g. commuting gates or unentangled qubits), $g(\theta)$ can have zero or near-zero eigenvalues. We apply Levenberg-Marquardt / Tikhonov regularization:
$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \left( g(\theta_t) + \lambda I \right)^{-1} \nabla \langle H \rangle(\theta_t)$$
This navigates along the steepest descent path on the Riemannian quantum state manifold, accelerating convergence across flat parameter valleys.

## 7. Shot Noise & Barren Plateaus

### 7.1 Shot Noise Scaling
When an observable $H$ is measured on a quantum computer using $S$ projective shots, the empirical average $\hat{E}_S = \frac{1}{S} \sum_{i=1}^S \lambda_i$ is a random variable. By the Central Limit Theorem:
$$\mathbb{E}[\hat{E}_S] = \langle H \rangle, \quad \operatorname{Var}[\hat{E}_S] = \frac{\sigma^2}{S}$$
The measurement standard error scales as $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{S})$, requiring $10^4$ to $10^6$ shots for precision $< 10^{-3}$.

### 7.2 Barren Plateau Phenomenon
McClean et al. (2018) proved that for deep parameterized circuits forming an approximate 2-design (Haar-random ensemble):
$$\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[ \frac{\partial \langle H \rangle}{\partial \theta_k} \right] = 0$$
$$\operatorname{Var}_{\theta}\left[ \frac{\partial \langle H \rangle}{\partial \theta_k} \right] \in \mathcal{O}\left( \frac{1}{2^n} \right)$$
As the number of qubits $n$ increases, the probability of finding a non-zero gradient descent direction vanishes exponentially. This implies that randomly initialized gradient-based optimization becomes exponentially hard on deep ansatzes.
